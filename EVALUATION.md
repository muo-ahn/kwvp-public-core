## A. Ambient push and whitelist follow-up

### A1. Broad ambient push

### Hypothesis

Automatically surfacing stored knowledge would meet the preregistered minimum of 30% HELPED and at most 20% NOISE.

### Experimental Setup

A historical implementation injected candidate wiki material into sessions. The initial main-channel comparison informed stopping that channel; a later sub-agent cohort had a preregistered evaluation window and thresholds. The underlying runtime and logs are not part of this public candidate.

### Measurement Definition

HELPED and NOISE were judge-assigned verdict categories over injection events; the broad-cohort verdicts were generated by an LLM judge, with no independent human review documented. The threshold was HELPED ≥30% and NOISE ≤20%. These labels are imperfect judgments, not direct measurements of time saved.

### Dataset / Sample

The initial comparison was documented as 17% HELPED and 59% NOISE. The sub-agent evaluation reported n=628 judgments across 44 sessions: 16.2% HELPED and 64.5% NOISE. Source: `docs/pull-quality-measurement.md`, results section dated 2026-08-10; `docs/verdict-10-subagent-2026-08-27.md`, §§1–2.

### Baseline / Control

The preregistered threshold served as the decision baseline. There was no randomized no-injection control in this cohort.

### Results

Both reported cohorts missed both thresholds. The larger sub-agent cohort did not approach the preregistered gate.

### Limitations

Verdicts are not randomized causal estimates. The older implementation, page mix, and channel behavior are specific to that period. Historical figures were not recalculated from event logs for this publication.

### Decision

Stop the evaluated sub-agent push configuration and require a new, prospective design before any broad resumption. This does not show that all push approaches are ineffective.

### Reproduction Instructions

The historical result cannot be reproduced from this public repository because its raw logs and runtime are intentionally absent. Check the arithmetic from the counts above. The synthetic candidate's `evaluate` command demonstrates a different, deterministic fixture evaluation and must not be represented as reproducing this result.

### Evidence Classification

**DOCUMENTED historical result; arithmetic checkable; not independently reproduced; causal conclusion INCONCLUSIVE.**

### A2. Selected whitelist follow-up

### Hypothesis

A narrowly selected whitelist of pages could perform better than the broad push population under the same verdict thresholds.

### Experimental Setup

A follow-up whitelist cohort was examined on 2026-09-22, two days before the scheduled 2026-09-24 decision date. It was a selected subset, not a randomized or prospectively held-out test of general resumption.

### Measurement Definition

HELPED and NOISE proportions were calculated among verdicts; preregistered thresholds were HELPED ≥30% and NOISE ≤20%.

### Dataset / Sample

The report records 84 judgments from 16 parent sessions: 54 HELPED (64.3%) and 11 NOISE (13.1%). Half of HELPED judgments (27/54) came from one session. Source: `docs/verdict-10-subagent-2026-08-27.md`, follow-up §§11–11.4, dated 2026-09-22; `docs/value-criteria-draft-2026-10-01.md`, §§25–41.

### Baseline / Control

The comparison was the prior 628-judgment broad cohort and the unchanged preregistered thresholds. There was no concurrent randomized control.

### Results

The selected subset crossed both thresholds descriptively. The two-day early read and one-session concentration materially weaken any broad interpretation.

### Limitations

Whitelist selection, repeated parent sessions, outcome concentration, shortened window, and lack of a randomized control create selection and temporal risks. The result does not justify pooling into later measurements. A later change in hook path on 2026-09-30 creates a distinct implementation period; it is not a continuation of this sample.

### Decision

Treat the observation as a narrow signal for prospective testing only. Do not describe it as evidence that the general channel resumed successfully.

### Reproduction Instructions

Historical reanalysis is unavailable without source logs. Verify only the published arithmetic: 54/84 = 64.3%, 11/84 = 13.1%, and 27/54 = 50%. The synthetic fixture evaluation is separate.

### Evidence Classification

**DOCUMENTED selected follow-up; arithmetic checkable; not independently reproduced; general efficacy INCONCLUSIVE.**

### A3. Holdout re-derivation

### Hypothesis

If stored knowledge prevents work from being re-derived, sessions without that knowledge should show REDERIVED judgments.

### Experimental Setup

A historical holdout arm was judged after the intervention window, using NOT_REDERIVED, UNCLEAR, and REDERIVED labels. The design intended to estimate whether work would be repeated without the injected knowledge.

### Measurement Definition

REDERIVED is the event-level indicator that the knowledge was independently re-derived; NOT_REDERIVED indicates no such evidence. UNCLEAR is retained rather than forced into either outcome.

### Dataset / Sample

Fourteen judgments across nine sessions: 13 NOT_REDERIVED, one UNCLEAR, zero REDERIVED. Source: `docs/l3-utility-measurement.md`, §8, dated 2026-10-02.

### Baseline / Control

The holdout arm is the comparison to the contemporaneous on arm (36 judgments: 11 HELPED, 7 USED_NEUTRAL, 18 NOISE). Assignment and session comparability do not support a strong causal estimate.

### Results

No REDERIVED event was observed in this small holdout sample. This is a directional observation, not proof that re-derivation is rare or that recall prevented it.

### Limitations

Small sample, nine sessions, model-based judgments without human cross-check, possible missing historical logs, and unclear counterfactual assignment. Zero observed events does not establish a zero underlying rate.

### Decision

Classify the holdout result as INCONCLUSIVE due to insufficient sample. Do not use it as a go/no-go proof for extraction automation.

### Reproduction Instructions

Raw records are excluded, so the historical judgment cannot be independently reproduced. The count and ratios are directly checkable from the documented aggregate only.

### Evidence Classification

**DOCUMENTED historical result; arithmetic checkable; not independently reproduced; INCONCLUSIVE.**

## B. Retrieval: pull carry-over and direct judgments

### Hypothesis

Reading wiki material is associated with later task output, and direct usefulness judgments might pass the same HELPED/NOISE thresholds as push.

### Experimental Setup

A deterministic probe counted actual read events and compared later lexical carry-over with a negative-control set of unread pages. A separate judge pass rated a random sample of 20 read events. The design was documented before results.

### Measurement Definition

Carry-over meant that at least one novel page token appeared after the read. The control applied the same lexical calculation to unread pages. The direct judgment labels were HELPED, NEUTRAL, and NOISE; the threshold was HELPED ≥30% and NOISE ≤20%.

### Dataset / Sample

The probe reported 41/53 read events with carry-over (77.4%), versus 24/53 controls (45.3%), a descriptive lift of 32.1 percentage points. The 20-event judgment sample reported 8 HELPED, 8 NEUTRAL, and 4 NOISE. Source: `docs/pull-quality-measurement.md`, §§5.5–5.6, dated 2026-08-10.

### Baseline / Control

The unread-page negative control was the baseline for lexical carry-over. The preregistered direct-judgment thresholds were the baseline for the 20-event sample.

### Results

The lexical difference is positive but the control is high, showing substantial topic-overlap noise. Direct judgments met the threshold exactly at the NOISE boundary; one additional NOISE judgment would have failed it.

### Limitations

Lexical carry-over is not usefulness. The control was a single sample, confidence intervals were not reported, and topic similarity can inflate both groups. The direct sample is small and judge-based. A later record superseded the 20-event snapshot: the all-sample report documented 24 HELPED, 17 NEUTRAL, and 12 NOISE among 53 judgments (45.3%, 32.1%, 22.6%), which misses the NOISE threshold. Keep the earlier sample as historical, not current summary.

### Decision

Do not claim pull usefulness from lexical lift alone. The expanded direct judgment record is inconclusive under the preregistered joint gate because NOISE exceeds 20%, even though HELPED exceeds 30%.

### Reproduction Instructions

The public repository lacks historical page text and read logs, so neither probe can be rerun. Check published arithmetic only. Use `PYTHONPATH=src python -m kwvp_core.cli --state /tmp/kwvp-demo evaluate examples/judgments.jsonl` for the separate synthetic demonstration; this is not historical reproduction.

### Evidence Classification

**DOCUMENTED historical measurements; not independently reproduced; usefulness conclusion INCONCLUSIVE.**

## C. Knowledge-to-skill promotion

### Hypothesis

Promoting repeated procedures into reusable skills should reduce the frequency of restating those procedures in later sessions.

### Experimental Setup

A preregistered before/after count compared topic-matching sessions before and after promotion for two topics. The post window began 2026-08-11. A 2026-09-30 review applied the preregistered decision rule and separately counted skill tool invocations.

### Measurement Definition

The source's regex counter counted sessions with a matching phrase, not the amount of procedural re-explanation. Skill invocation was counted separately from matching user utterances. A future measure must distinguish calling a skill from spelling out the procedure again.

### Dataset / Sample

For the clipboard-command topic: 10/285 sessions before (3.5%) and 5/366 after (1.4%); no matching utterance in the most recent three weeks, with about 50 skill calls recorded. For peer review: 10/285 before (3.5%) and 13/366 after (3.6%); three skill calls versus 13 sessions containing a matching utterance. Source: `docs/midreview-2026-09-30.md`, §§1–1.2, dated 2026-09-30; `TODO.md`, § “2026-10-17 KWVP wiki curation wave,” lines 217–223.

### Baseline / Control

The baseline was the pre-promotion session rate for each topic. There was no randomized comparison topic or stable exposure denominator.

### Results

The clipboard topic declined descriptively and met the preregistered convergence rule. Peer-review mentions did not decline; the source diagnosed low skill invocation as a likely trigger problem. This is a mixed topic-level outcome.

### Limitations

Different time windows, usage volume, regex matching, and no concurrent control prevent causal attribution to promotion. A matching phrase may be only a short skill invocation, not procedural re-explanation. The clipboard result may reflect changing demand; the peer-review result is confounded by non-invocation.

### Decision

Do not claim that promotion generally reduces repeated work. Preserve the topic-specific signal and redesign the next measurement to separate skill calls from procedural restatement.

### Reproduction Instructions

The public package omits source session records, so the historical counts cannot be rerun. The published fractions are arithmetically checkable only. Synthetic candidate evaluation is unrelated to this before/after analysis.

### Evidence Classification

**DOCUMENTED historical counts; not independently reproduced; causal conclusion INCONCLUSIVE.**

## D. Reliability: blinded relabeling of prior HELPED judgments

### Hypothesis

A blind reviewer applying a revised rubric would confirm that earlier HELPED examples genuinely reflected useful knowledge.

### Experimental Setup

On 2026-10-17, the full set of 14 earlier HELPED judgments in scope was blindly relabeled. The reviewer classified whether the candidate knowledge was reflected in the downstream work. The 17 October date is the report date.

### Measurement Definition

Categories were reflected, unrelated, or unknown. Agreement is reported against the previous relabeling pass, not against the source batch from which the 14 judgments were drawn.

### Dataset / Sample

On 2026-10-17, all 14 earlier HELPED judgments in scope were blindly relabeled: one reflected, 13 unrelated, and zero unknown. Agreement with the previous relabeling was 10/14; the positive item changed. Source: `TODO.md`, “2026-10-17 KWVP wiki curation wave,” WC2 first step, and its cited decision record summarized there.

### Baseline / Control

The comparison was an earlier relabeling pass on the same items. There was no independent multi-rater control or random sample of all historical judgments.

### Results

The review did not corroborate a broad set of prior helpful judgments. The discrepant positive item and limited agreement make the direction informative but unstable.

### Limitations

The audited set was limited to 14 prior HELPED judgments, only one relabeler was used, and the rubric changed over time. No causal claims or population estimates are warranted. Version 4 results must remain separate from version 3 aggregates.

### Decision

Use the result as a reason to audit labels and keep rubric versions separate, not as a corrected population HELPED rate.

### Reproduction Instructions

No source items or private review records are included. The aggregate cannot be independently reproduced here; inspect the source-relative summary locator only. Do not infer undocumented item-level mapping.

### Evidence Classification

**DOCUMENTED audit summary; selected sample and single relabeler; not independently reproduced; population conclusion INCONCLUSIVE.**
